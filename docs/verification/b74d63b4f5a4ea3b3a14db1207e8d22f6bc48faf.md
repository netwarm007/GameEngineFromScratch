## Corrected Splash behavior

The text now finishes its 6.208-second animation at 90 degrees upright and stays
there. The extra return-to-flat segment is removed. Only after the last key does
the ball appear at its adjusted starting position.

The ball retains its speed, friction, and restitution; its launch direction is
rotated to follow the text's final heading. Its old planar motion constraint is
removed. Bullet drives free three-dimensional motion against the actual static
text triangle mesh, without hidden supports or scripted trajectory corrections.

`TEST_PhysicsSceneIntegrationTest`, `TEST_SceneServicesTest`, and
`TEST_OgexParserTest` passed on all three platforms. The physics integration test
checks the actual Splash asset, including:

- At 30, 60, and 144 FPS animation sampling, the text rotates while the ball
  stays hidden at its authored spawn, with no physics bodies during the
  introduction.
- The introduction ends at the original 6.208-second time key, without an
  additional settling segment. The final text transform is 90 degrees upright.
- Immediately before the final key the ball remains hidden; at the key both
  bodies are created and the ball appears at its authored starting position.
- Only the ball and actual text have collision bodies, and the ball has no
  planar motion constraint.
- The ball reaches longitudinal coordinate greater than 5.5 along the text's
  rotated heading within 25 simulated seconds and rebounds at least three
  times. Its center stays more than its 0.3-unit radius above the text baseline
  and less than one radius from the text's longitudinal vertical plane.
- The text's rendered transform stays at the upright endpoint throughout
  traversal. Subsequent animation/sequence updates neither flatten the text
  nor respawn the ball.
- Reset, scene reload, skipping past the endpoint, and leaving Splash preserve
  the intended sequence lifecycle.

Before changing the asset, the new regression failed against the old
return-to-flat version. Local Windows tests passed after the correction,
including five consecutive fresh-process physics regression runs. A local
actual-scene traversal took 223 fixed physics steps with five rebounds; these
measurements are local observations, not an assertion that every platform has
an identical trajectory.

## Scope and limitations

- CI screenshots are individual post-handoff instants, not a recording of the
  entire animation or every collision. The phase and traversal tests provide
  the automated behavioral evidence; local phase captures supplement them.
- Animation uses elapsed time while Bullet advances a fixed step per tick.
  Different render rates can produce different wall-clock traversal speeds and
  screenshot positions. Equal wall-clock ball positions were not verified.
- The trajectory depends on the mesh, initial conditions, material values,
  Bullet version, and floating-point behavior. Modified assets and arbitrary
  physics configurations are not covered by this record.
- Local captures retained pre-existing missing terrain-texture warnings.
  These warnings also occurred before this correction and were not changed.
- The rendered configurations were Windows D3D12, Linux OpenGL, and macOS Metal.
  Other backends and disabled Android are not covered.

This record verifies only the source revision named above. Its documentation-only
follow-up does not establish a newly tested source revision. Other branches,
later source changes, and merge revisions require their own complete CI results.
